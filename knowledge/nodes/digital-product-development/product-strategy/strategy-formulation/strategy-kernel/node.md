## Definition

The structure that Richard Rumelt finds under each good strategy. It has three parts. A diagnosis explains the nature of the challenge and picks out the aspects of the situation that matter most. A guiding policy is the overall approach chosen to deal with the obstacles that the diagnosis names. Coherent actions are steps that support the guiding policy and reinforce each other. A strategy is coherent action backed by an argument. It is not an ambition, a vision, or a plan of targets.

## Notes

Rumelt's example is Nvidia in the late 1990s. The diagnosis was that the company was losing the performance race. The guiding policy was to release a new chip every 6 months, while the industry released one every 18 months. The coherent actions included three development teams on overlapping schedules and large investments in simulation. A growth target such as "20% revenue growth and a 20% margin" is a goal, not a strategy, because it says nothing about how to get there.

The limit of `3` objectives is stricter than Rumelt, who asks for one or a very few. An objective that is as hard to reach as the original challenge adds nothing to the strategy.
