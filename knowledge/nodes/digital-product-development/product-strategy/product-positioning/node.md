## Definition

How a product leads at delivering something that a well-defined set of customers cares a lot about, in the words of April Dunford. Positioning sets the context in which customers judge a product. It tells them who the product competes with, which features it has, who it is for, and what it costs. Positioning is not the messaging, the tagline, the brand story, or the vision. Those build on it.

## Notes

Dunford rejects the fill-in positioning statement as a way to find positioning. It assumes that each blank has one known answer, while most products can sit in several categories, against several competitors, for several kinds of customer. A finished positioning can still be written as a statement. The rule is about where the work starts.

When a product is called a "CRM", buyers assume its competitor, its buyer, its features, and its price before they read further. Good positioning makes those assumptions true.
