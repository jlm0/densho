## Definition

A way through a difficulty: an approach that overcomes an obstacle or answers a challenge. Richard Rumelt calls a strategy good when it focuses effort and resources on the few points that decide the outcome, and bad when it avoids that choice. Bad strategy shows four signs. It does not face the challenge. It states goals in place of a way to reach them. It sets objectives that are a long list of tasks or a restatement of the challenge. It hides the absence of thought behind fluff.

## Notes

Rumelt defines fluff as a restatement of the obvious with buzzwords that pose as expertise. His example is a bank whose stated strategy was "customer-centric intermediation", which meant only that it was a bank. Template-style planning fills in a vision, a mission, values, and goals, then calls the goals strategies. Choice is the hard part: a group that must reach consensus on conflicting options often settles on a statement that chooses nothing.
