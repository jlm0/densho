## Definition

The hierarchy of the brands of one company: how the parent company, its subsidiaries, its products, and its services relate. Alina Wheeler names three types:

- Monolithic: one strong master brand. Each extension uses the parent identity with a generic descriptor, such as FedEx Office.
- Endorsed: each product or division has its own market presence and gains from the visible endorsement of the parent, such as iPad and Apple.
- Pluralistic: a set of well-known brands. The parent name stays in the background or is known only to investors.

Most large companies mix the types.

## Notes

Wheeler lists the questions that decide the type for a new product or an acquisition: the benefit of the parent name, the need to keep the new entity apart from the parent, the risk that co-branding confuses customers, and whether to keep the equity of an acquired name.
