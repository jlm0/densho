## Definition

A view of demand in which people "hire" a product to make progress. A job is the progress that a person tries to make in a given circumstance. Clayton Christensen stresses that each job has functional, social, and emotional sides, and that a change of circumstance changes the job. Tony Ulwick stresses that a job stays stable while products change and that it names no solution. He writes a job statement as a verb, the object of the verb, and an optional clarifier of the context, such as "pass on life lessons to children". Any solution that people use to get the job done competes with the product, not only products of the same kind.

## Notes

The two schools share the core idea and differ in method. Christensen's school looks for the circumstances and the forces that push a person toward a choice or away from it, and finds them in the story of a real purchase. Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation breaks the core functional job into steps and collects the measures that customers use to judge each step, which he calls desired outcomes.

The milkshake example shows the competitor rule: morning buyers hired a milkshake for a long drive, so its competitors were bananas and bagels, not other milkshakes.
