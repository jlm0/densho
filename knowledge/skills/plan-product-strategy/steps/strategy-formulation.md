## Inputs

- The scope of the strategy: a product, a team, or a company, and the period that it covers
- The facts of the situation: the users, the market, the competitors, the capabilities, the constraints, and the results so far
- Each goal or target that the strategy serves

## Procedure

1. Collect the facts of the situation. If the diagnosis needs a fact that the inputs do not give, ask the person for it.
2. Write the diagnosis. Name the main challenge and the few factors that make it hard.
3. Write the guiding policy: the approach that deals with the obstacles of the diagnosis. State what the policy rules out.
4. Write the coherent actions. For each action, state how it supports the guiding policy and the other actions.
5. List the goals and the work that the strategy sets aside.
6. Read the draft for the four signs of bad strategy: no named challenge, goals in place of a strategy, a long list of objectives, and fluff. Correct each one that you find.

## Output

One strategy document with four sections: the diagnosis, the guiding policy, the coherent actions, and what the strategy sets aside. Each goal or target stays a goal, apart from the strategy.

## Exit gate

Check the document against each directive of this step and of the skill, and report each one that it misses. A reader who did not write the strategy can state the challenge, the approach, and the first actions from the document alone.
