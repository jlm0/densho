## Definition

You aren't gonna need it: a capability that a team presumes the software needs in the future is not built now. Martin Fowler calls such a capability a presumptive feature. Its costs are the cost to build it, the cost of delay to the features that the team needs now, and the cost of carry on each later change. And the team is likely to be wrong about what the future needs.
