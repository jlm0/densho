## Definition

A visual map, from Teresa Torres, of the paths that a team can take to reach a desired outcome. The outcome sits at the root. Under it, the opportunity space holds the unmet needs, pain points, and desires of customers that drive the outcome when the product addresses them. Under each opportunity, the solution space holds the products, features, or services that address it. Assumption tests at the bottom show which solutions work. Torres separates three kinds of outcome: a business outcome measures the health of the business, a product outcome measures a behavior or a sentiment of customers in the product, and a traction metric measures the use of one feature.

## Notes

"I want to go out to eat" sounds like an opportunity, but it is a solution in disguise: going out is the only way to address it. Behind it sit needs such as "I don't have time to cook", and each of those has many solutions.

Torres assesses an opportunity on four sets of factors: how many customers it affects and how often, its effect on the position of the product in the market, its fit with the strategy of the company, and its importance to customers against their satisfaction with current solutions. Effort belongs to the solutions, and an opportunity that looks hard can have a simple solution.

A team starts a new tree when the direction of its outcome changes, not when only the target number changes. Torres revisits the opportunity space after each `3` to `4` interviews. The number `3` in the solution rule is her working number. She asks for more than one solution, and for a comparison when the solution carries risk.
