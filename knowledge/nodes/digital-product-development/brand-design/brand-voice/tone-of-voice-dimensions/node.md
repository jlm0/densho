## Definition

The four scales on which Kate Moran of Nielsen Norman Group describes the tone of any content:

- Formal to casual
- Serious to funny
- Respectful to irreverent
- Matter-of-fact to enthusiastic

A tone profile is one point on each scale. In NN/g surveys, users noticed when only the tone of a page changed. The tone changed how friendly, how trustworthy, and how desirable the brand seemed. Trust predicted the willingness to recommend the brand much more strongly than friendliness did.

## Notes

Tone words refine the profile, for example "playful" or "quirky". NN/g advises a handful of them, so that writers can follow the guidance. Anti-tone words name the tones that the brand avoids, such as pedantic for a university that wants to sound authoritative.

To test the tone, ask users to choose words that describe the content and compare them with the tone words, or ask them to rate the content on each scale. The differences in the NN/g survey were small, about half a point to one point on a five-point scale, so a test needs both numbers and the comments of users.

In the NN/g study, a playful insurance page seemed friendlier than a serious one, but users trusted it less and were no more willing to recommend it. A casual but serious bank page did better than a formal one on friendliness, trust, and willingness to recommend. Clever headlines on a security site failed to tell users what came below them.
