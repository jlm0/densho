## Definition

Kevin Lane Keller's customer-based brand equity model. A brand is built in four steps, and each step answers a question that customers ask of it:

1. Identity, "Who are you?": brand salience, the depth and breadth of awareness.
2. Meaning, "What are you?": brand performance and brand imagery, which must be strong, favorable, and unique associations.
3. Responses, "What do I think or feel about you?": brand judgments and brand feelings, which must be positive and come to mind readily.
4. Relationships, "What about you and me?": brand resonance, which must be intense and active.

The six building blocks form a pyramid with salience at the base and resonance at the top. The left side is the rational route, from performance to judgments. The right side is the emotional route, from imagery to feelings.

## Notes

Each step needs the step below it. Meaning cannot form before people know the brand, responses cannot form before the meaning, and a relationship cannot form before the responses.

Depth of awareness is how easily people recall or recognize the brand. Breadth is the range of situations in which it comes to mind. Awareness includes knowing the category that the brand belongs to and the needs that it serves. Keller explains the failed online brands of his time this way: costly campaigns taught people a name but not the business behind it.

An association helps only in that order. A unique association matters only if customers value it, and a valued association matters only if it is strong enough for customers to recall it. Strong brands appeal to both the head and the heart, so they build both routes of the pyramid.

Resonance has four parts: behavioral loyalty, attitudinal attachment, a sense of community, and active engagement. The candidate measures in Keller's report start with what brands people can name for a category, and only then ask whether they have heard of a brand.
